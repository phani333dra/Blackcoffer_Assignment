AI for Water Utilities: How Artificial Intelligence Can Reduce Water Losses, Improve Service Reliability and Optimize Operations

Introduction

Water utilities manage the collection, treatment, distribution and delivery of drinking water, alongside wastewater collection, treatment and discharge where these services fall within their responsibilities.

Their operations involve reservoirs, treatment plants, pumping stations, pipelines, storage tanks, meters, wastewater networks, laboratories, field crews, billing systems and customer service.

Water losses, infrastructure deterioration, energy-intensive pumping, changing water demand, treatment variability and aging assets can increase costs and affect service reliability.

Artificial Intelligence can help water utilities build more efficient, resilient and data-driven water systems through predictive analytics, intelligent monitoring and operational automation.

AI can analyze flow and pressure measurements, water-quality readings, SCADA data, customer consumption, weather forecasts, maintenance records and financial information to improve performance across the water cycle.

1. AI Water Demand Forecasting & Supply Planning

Water utilities must forecast demand across distribution networks, treatment plants, storage reservoirs and customer segments.

AI can analyze:

Historical water consumption

Seasonal demand patterns

Weather conditions

Population and customer growth

Industrial consumption

Reservoir levels

Irrigation demand

Meter readings

Historical service disruptions

Machine learning models can forecast demand at different time horizons, helping utilities plan production, storage, pumping and distribution.

For example, an AI system could anticipate increased water demand during a period of unusually high temperatures and help operators evaluate whether treatment and distribution capacity will be sufficient.

Potential impact: Better supply planning, more efficient resource allocation and improved operational readiness.

2. AI Leak Detection & Non-Revenue Water Reduction

Water losses can occur through leaking pipes, faulty connections, overflowing tanks, inaccurate meters and unauthorized consumption.

AI can analyze:

Flow measurements

Pressure readings

Smart meter data

Minimum night flows

District metered area balances

Pipe characteristics

Historical leakage incidents

Water production versus billed consumption

It can identify unusual consumption patterns and prioritize network zones for field inspection.

For example, a distribution zone with unusually high overnight flow and unexplained pressure changes could be flagged for investigation.

AI can also help distinguish normal demand fluctuations from patterns that may indicate continuous leakage or metering problems.

Potential impact: Better leakage visibility, more targeted inspections and improved control over water losses.

An anomaly is not proof of a leak or unauthorized connection. Utilities should verify alerts using field measurements and established investigation procedures.

3. AI Water Quality Monitoring & Treatment Optimization

Water utilities must maintain water quality throughout treatment, storage and distribution.

AI can analyze sensor readings, laboratory results and treatment-process data to identify abnormal patterns.

Applications include monitoring:

Turbidity

pH

Temperature

Conductivity

Chlorine residuals

Dissolved oxygen where relevant

Treatment chemical consumption

Filter performance

Water-quality complaints

AI can identify changes that may require sampling, inspection or adjustments to treatment processes.

For wastewater treatment facilities, AI can also support analysis of aeration, nutrient removal, process loading and treatment performance.

Potential impact: Earlier identification of process deviations, more efficient monitoring and better visibility into treatment operations.

AI must not replace required sampling, validated analytical tests or drinking-water quality controls. Critical treatment and disinfection decisions must remain within approved procedures and under qualified operational oversight.

4. AI Predictive Maintenance for Pipes, Pumps & Treatment Equipment

Water utilities operate extensive infrastructure that must remain reliable despite aging equipment, varying loads and changing environmental conditions.

AI can analyze operational telemetry, inspections and maintenance records to identify potential equipment problems.

Applications include:

Pump and motor condition monitoring

Pipeline failure-risk analysis

Valve and actuator monitoring

Reservoir equipment inspection

Filter and membrane performance analysis

Compressor and blower monitoring

Transformer condition monitoring

Maintenance prioritization

For example, AI can combine pump vibration, temperature, operating hours and hydraulic performance to flag equipment that may require further inspection.

It can also help utilities move from fixed schedules toward condition-informed maintenance planning.

Potential impact: Reduced unplanned downtime, improved maintenance planning and better asset availability.

5. AI Hydraulic Network Modeling & Pressure Optimization

Water distribution networks contain interconnected pipes, pumps, valves, reservoirs and storage tanks.

AI can help utilities analyze network behavior using hydraulic models, operational telemetry and historical demand.

Applications include:

Pressure anomaly detection

Pump scheduling optimization

Network capacity analysis

Water hammer risk assessment

Storage-level planning

Distribution-zone balancing

Network expansion scenarios

Energy-efficient pressure management

For example, a utility can compare alternative pumping schedules and pressure settings to understand their likely effects on energy consumption, service reliability and leakage risk.

AI can also help identify locations where pressure management or pipe rehabilitation deserves further engineering review.

Potential impact: Improved network performance, more efficient pumping and better visibility into infrastructure constraints.

Final operating settings must be validated against hydraulic models, minimum service pressures, fire-flow requirements and applicable engineering standards.

6. AI Wastewater Collection & Treatment Optimization

Wastewater utilities must manage sewer networks, pumping stations, treatment plants, stormwater interactions and treatment performance.

AI can analyze flow sensors, rainfall data, equipment telemetry and process measurements to help identify:

Unusual wastewater inflows

Potential sewer blockages

Pumping-station anomalies

Inflow and infiltration patterns

Overflow risks

Aeration inefficiencies

Treatment-process deviations

Sludge production trends

For example, AI can combine rainfall forecasts and sewer flow measurements to identify network locations that may require additional monitoring during heavy rain.

At treatment plants, machine learning can help operators evaluate aeration requirements, energy use and process performance.

Potential impact: Better treatment-plant visibility, improved maintenance planning and earlier identification of potential network problems.

AI predictions should support established overflow prevention, environmental monitoring and emergency-response procedures.

7. AI Energy Optimization for Water Infrastructure

Pumping, aeration, filtration, treatment and distribution can account for substantial operating energy consumption.

AI can analyze:

Electricity consumption

Pump efficiency

Flow and pressure requirements

Treatment-plant load

Reservoir levels

Demand forecasts

Tariff structures where applicable

Equipment operating schedules

It can recommend more efficient pump combinations, operating schedules and treatment workflows within approved constraints.

For example, an AI system could compare pumping scenarios to identify opportunities to reduce energy consumption while maintaining required supply pressure and reservoir levels.

Potential impact: Lower energy consumption, improved equipment utilization and reduced operating costs.

Energy optimization must preserve treatment requirements, hydraulic limits, equipment operating envelopes and service reliability.

8. AI Smart Meter Analytics & Customer Service

Smart meters and digital customer systems provide utilities with more detailed information about consumption, billing and service conditions.

AI can help analyze:

Unusual household or commercial consumption

Meter reading anomalies

Potential meter faults

Billing discrepancies

Repeated customer complaints

Service interruption patterns

Consumption trends

Customer support requests

AI assistants can help customers understand their bills, report leaks, request service appointments and receive updates about planned interruptions.

For example, an AI assistant can summarize a customer’s consumption history and explain approved billing information while routing disputes or unusual account issues to authorized staff.

Potential impact: Faster customer support, improved billing investigations and better use of meter data.

Utilities should protect customer consumption records through appropriate privacy safeguards, data minimization and access controls.

9. AI Infrastructure Planning & Water Utility Digital Twins

Water utilities must plan upgrades and replacements across assets that may have different ages, conditions, criticality and maintenance histories.

AI-supported digital twins can combine:

Geographic information system (GIS) records

Hydraulic models

SCADA data

Asset registers

Inspection findings

Customer demand

Maintenance costs

Historical failures

Utilities can use these models to simulate alternative operating scenarios and evaluate potential infrastructure investments.

Applications include:

Pipe replacement prioritization

Storage capacity planning

Treatment-plant expansion

Network resilience assessment

Failure consequence analysis

Capital investment planning

Emergency response simulation

Asset life-cycle cost forecasting

For example, AI can help compare pipe rehabilitation options based on recorded condition, failure history, service consequences and estimated replacement costs.

Potential impact: Better capital planning, improved asset prioritization and stronger infrastructure resilience.

AI should support validated engineering models and professional judgment rather than independently determine infrastructure safety or investment decisions.
